## Current premise and storytelling objective

The player is Director Scut, a corporate problem solver with a reputation for venting problems, operating remotely through MOTHER-7's supervisory terminal connected to industrial carrier THETA-9. Something has happened aboard; reports conflict and a disturbance seems to move, but nothing establishes its nature, origin, or even that it is a single phenomenon. He relies on MOTHER-7 for observations, communications, recommendations, and mediated access to controls, and must decide what the problem is, how to respond, and what to conclude. Success has many forms; the game recognizes consequences rather than a single correct solution. See plan Sections 1–5 for the full premise, tone, and scenario strands.

The session begins with Scut already connected and MOTHER-7's concise briefing, not a login or handover. The briefing introduces uncertainty and actionable investigations without naming a culprit. Scut discovers capabilities through `help`, `menu`, and operational questions to MOTHER-7. No essential action should require guessing an undocumented command or a developer command.

The simulation determines physical events and consequences. MOTHER-7 interprets, selects, and may misrepresent what Scut sees. Decisions based on those interpretations alter the simulation, creating new consequences and evidence. Her motives remain ambiguous; any eventual revelation must be discoverable and causally supported by prior events, observations and choices. The final disposition is Scut's **director's cut**, evaluated against the independently preserved authoritative record (plan §3, §7).

**Central principle:** The simulation establishes what happens. MOTHER-7 constructs an interpretation of what happens. Scut acts on that interpretation and changes what happens next.

The technical feedback loop this project already demonstrates is:

**Simulation event -> causal narrative context -> MOTHER-7 interpretation -> player decision -> validated action -> changed simulation -> updated narrative.**

Player decisions must materially influence outcomes. Decisions include accepting, refusing, or challenging a recommendation, making a discovery, or consequential inaction. These can affect subsequent narrative behavior even when no simulation action is committed. The game must be fully playable through the deterministic offline MOTHER-7; the optional local LLM provides contextual expression and suggestions only and does not author authoritative state or physical consequences.

### Causal context and evidence model (general target)

Treat these as three distinct knowledge perspectives, linked rather than collapsed into one prompt:

- **Authoritative scenario truth:** model state and hidden scenario facts, including physical consequences and operational state owned by the subsystem. This is the canonical world record.
- **MOTHER-7's knowledge and concealed agenda:** only the operational context selected for the director plus any authored private narrative objective. The objective may guide recommendations intended to influence Scut, but is not automatically disclosed and does not change scenario facts or grant the model physical authority.
- **Scut's player-visible knowledge:** claims and observations actually delivered through the terminal, inspected evidence, discoveries, and the player's own decisions. Do not equate this with the full authoritative record or MOTHER-7's private objective.

Retain significant causal events beyond simulation mutations: recommendations and claims, operator acceptance/refusal/challenge, discoveries, and inaction when its consequences are observable and narratively relevant. A refusal is an event even when it queues no `sim_action`; a subsequent narrative response may change because of that refusal without pretending the simulation changed. Avoid recording every absence of input as a consequential event.

Beyond Experiment 1, a general layer should make significant causal relationships queryable across scenarios and several minutes, not merely concatenate the full event log. Context selection must be bounded, relevant to the current request, provenance-preserving, reset-safe, and explicit about stale or missing evidence. The appropriate record schema, retention horizon, and separation of hidden authoring data from model-visible context remain implementation decisions.

## Experiment 1: operational continuity (2026-11-14)

Implemented scope: one authored supply interruption in existing subsystem-owned operational authority, with simulated oxygen consequences. This is not a hidden-agenda storyline or a general narrative memory system. The architecture table above retains the broader target; this section defines the implemented episode boundary.

**Play (existing Experiment 1 fixture):** initialize with `start_experiment`, then enter `continuity_incident`. This opts into one REACTOR_CORE supply interruption per reset and enables AI-link. The authoritative power overlay becomes offline and measured oxygen starts at 64%. MOTHER-7 reports those readings and recommends `power reactor_core nominal`. Alternatively enter `refuse recommendation` or `investigate incident`. Investigation captures read-only power/O2 evidence; it does not diagnose an unauthored cause. Use `incident status` later, including after unrelated exchanges. Reset to compare paths. Nominal power restores oxygen through existing `StepCrisis()` rates; refusal leaves supply offline. Oxygen does not recover merely because MOTHER says it will.

`FCausalContextRecord` retains ID, authoritative event tick, parent/request IDs, provenance, kind, bounded text and player visibility. Maximum 64 records, maximum 384 characters per record: the incident and recommendation are pinned; ordinary inputs, delivered claims and samples are evicted before significant choices/outcomes. At saturation of significant records the oldest non-anchor is evicted. Missing parents remain IDs rather than invented summaries; snapshots disclose eviction count and retention scope. This is per-run memory, cleared on experiment reset, not a save-game ledger. It does not ingest every historical event/cue or hidden agenda.

The incident is recorded from authoritative scenario setup and reported through `RecordCue()`/`LogEvent()`. Explicit decisions enter the journal before request capture. Accepted/rejected action outcomes retain request relationships and exact commit ticks from `RecordDirectorOutcome()`/`CommandRecords`. `ServiceDirector()` samples actual reactor power/O2 at most once per 90 simulation ticks outside stepping. Parent links on samples mean observed after a decision, not proof that refusal physically caused a change. Delivered generated prose is separately labeled `mother7_claim`; selected measurements and decisions never promote generated text into authority. The six-message transcript remains bounded as before; incident-only model queries omit unrelated conversational turns and rely on selected causal evidence.

`MakeContextSnapshot()` includes the bounded episode journal plus an `operational_episode` projection: historical incident, recommendation claim, latest Scut decision, latest validation, current measured tick/power/O2. The existing Ollama request selects the two episode anchors and at most ten recent significant records, carries their provenance plus this concise current-state projection, and narrows the episode reply/restoration tool schemas. Provider instructions distinguish old readings from current telemetry, require non-mutating refusal/investigation/status replies, and forbid invented notifications/repairs. The offline provider can recall the same episode and current readings. Only existing validated typed actions commit at the next fixed tick, with existing commit-time revalidation. Replay still consumes resolved actions without inference; reproducing this opt-in episode requires the same scenario setup before action replay.

## Proposed self-contained Windows inference distribution

**Status: proposed stretch opportunity, not implemented or verified, and outside the approved Chunk A–C gameplay sequence.** Per the plan (§6, §10), packaging and distribution are not part of this sequence and the bundled runtime/model are optional. The game-jam target, if separately authorized, is a self-contained Windows distribution of approximately **2.0 GB installed**, containing the game, inference runtime, and model. Keep the simulation-grounded storytelling loop and playable deterministic fallback as the product priority; packaging the LLM must not become a prerequisite for starting or playing the game.

### Candidate architecture

Package the Unreal game with a pinned Windows `llama-server.exe` build from [`llama.cpp`](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp) and the preferred [`Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct-GGUF`](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct-GGUF) model at Q4_K_M quantization. Stage the GGUF and runtime dependencies as filesystem files beside the packaged game rather than assuming a third-party process can load a model from inside an Unreal `.pak`. The server exposes the OpenAI-compatible chat-completions API already used by `FLocalHttpShipAIIntentProvider`; typed directive parsing and validation remain the authority boundary.

The host must bind to loopback only. A game-instance-scoped host owner is the proposed lifecycle owner: start one child server for the application, wait for a bounded readiness check, then configure the existing provider to its localhost endpoint. World-level simulation subsystems attach to that endpoint and cancel their own requests on teardown; they must not launch duplicate servers during map changes. Avoid collisions with a user's Ollama or another process, and stop only the child process the game itself started. On application exit, request graceful shutdown, wait for a bounded interval, then terminate the owned child if necessary. If runtime startup, readiness, or inference fails, report it and continue with the deterministic provider without blocking fixed-step simulation. Current `DefaultGame.ini` configures an Ollama endpoint but does not establish a connection; the packaged launch/ownership path does not exist yet.

## P2-02b implementation - 2026-11-14

`IShipAIIntentProvider::Request` receives a value-only `FShipAIRequest` and a completion callback, replacing synchronous `(Command, Pretext)` translation. `FShipAIDirective` contains `RequestId`, `Generation`, `ObservationTick`, typed `Kind`/arguments and bounded `Pretext`. The deterministic offline provider handles explicit operational grammar, role/name routing, camera/widgets, reassurance and snapshot-based status evidence. `bAILink` selects conversational director routing; the ordinary CLI retains its established authoritative command path. The header's LINKED label denotes an explicitly configured HTTP transport selection, not a health guarantee; unconfigured AI-LINK remains LOCAL.

`ServiceDirector` dispatches providers, handles deadlines and delivers presentation notifications outside `FixedStep` (subsystem Tick, manual AdvanceSteps driver and presentation Tick). Pending requests, accepted actions and presentation delivery queues are each capped at 32. Both providers pass through one validator for identity/generation, observation age (450 ticks), enum/argument bounds, lifecycle/uplink/target eligibility and queue capacity. Generation cancellation on reset/deinit and closing requests before dispatch reject late/duplicate outcomes. A primary failure/schema rejection/timeout uses the original immutable request snapshot for deterministic fallback, once. Rejections are recorded and surfaced as SYSTEM prose. Keep helper declarations and lifecycle state in `SDirectOrScutWorkstationWidget.h`, implementations in its `.cpp`; no new numbered source units. `BuildCompartmentOutline` accepts stable room IDs 0-7 and local widget size, returns a closed outline from authored canonical room topology, and returns empty output for invalid rooms/nonpositive size. Cache by room/size; transform authoritative room positions into schematic space without exposing hidden ground truth as an unmediated HUD. Bound output to 128 vertices per outline. Filled convex pieces use prevalidated triangulation plus `MakeCustomVerts`; do not triangulate arbitrary concave geometry every paint. `BuildReticlePoints` rejects nonfinite input, clamps radius nonnegative and count to 1-64, clamps gap below the sector angle, and caches at most 32 points per arc. `MakeLines` draws each arc independently with antialiasing; native drawing owns rasterization, not a CPU per-pixel SDF loop.

Use linear interpolation and constant extrapolation; these are proposed phosphor art values, not extracted Loom keys. `EvaluatePhosphorColor` clamps finite input to [0,1] (nonfinite becomes 0) and calls native `GetLinearColorValue`. No replacement rich-curve evaluator, LUT asset, or Loom curve pointer is needed. CPU evaluation drives Slate colors and MID `PhosphorTint`; the minimal material does not silently claim to evaluate the entire four-key curve per pixel.

### Corrections and exit gates

Do not mechanically port extracted snippets. `MF_Loom_LineMin`, polygon and triangle snippets divide by squared edge length without a degenerate guard; in-place rotation updates X before computing Y and only accepts positive angles in several variants. Compute both coordinates from the original vector and accept signed rotations. Several min accumulators begin at 1, which caps distances; use valid first-shape initialization. Iterative SDF repetition is replaced by bounded cached CPU geometry for this UI.

Bezier snippets disagree on `sqrt(-p)` vs. `sqrt(-ky-kx*kx)`, test two vs. three roots, and vary denominator/acos guards. Native spline/polyline rendering avoids that solver entirely. The extracted smooth-min uses weights opposite the usual min-preserving blend: verify endpoints before reuse, and handle `k<=epsilon` as hard min; smooth-min is currently out of scope. Radial snippets also have differing outputs (filled annulus vs. angular-only stripes); verify connected output before selecting a reference. Procedural noise comments assert IQ/MIT provenance, which is a lead, not proof of redistribution rights.
